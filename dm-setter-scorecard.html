01

Role Definition & Boundaries

The setter runs the front of the revenue process: start qualified conversations, sort people correctly, close the low-ticket product (TPB) directly, and hand the high-ticket product (Academy / B.O.S.S.) to a closer as a booked, qualified, confirmed appointment. That is the whole job. Everything else is someone else's.

The setter OWNS

  • Initiating qualified new conversations from approved sources
  • Working warm inbound (comments, story replies, DMs) within SLA
  • Two-way conversation and qualification
  • Closing TPB directly ($97/mo) in the DM
  • Booking + confirming qualified Academy/B.O.S.S. appointments
  • Follow-up until a yes, a no, or a disqualify
  • CRM accuracy: stage, source, owner, next action, outcome

The setter does NOT own

  • Closing Academy / B.O.S.S. calls (closer) unless formally assigned + approved
  • Generating demand / ad spend / content (Marketing)
  • Onboarding, fulfillment, retention, member questions (Customer Success)
  • Community moderation, engagement, events (Community Manager)
  • Pricing, offer changes, refunds decisions (Founder / Ops)
  • Lead volume. If the machine does not create conversations, that is not the setter's miss.

TPB vs Academy handling, in one rule: the setter is paid to make the correct recommendation, not the most expensive one. TPB is closed in the DM. Academy/B.O.S.S. is never pitched or priced in the DM beyond a one-line frame; it is booked to a call. Qualify for fit first, product second.

Definitions & Qualification Ladder

Every KPI below depends on these being airtight. If "conversation" or "qualified" is fuzzy, the scorecard gets inflated and the comp plan gets disputed. One definition each, no interpretation room.

StagePrecise definition (what makes it count)
ContactOne outbound personalized first message sent to a net-new, approved-source prospect. A mass/identical blast does not count.
LeadA contact (or inbound) that matches ICP signals: breeds dogs or is actively starting, English-speaking, reachable, not a competitor/minor/spam.
Two-way conversationProspect sent at least 2 substantive replies (not just an emoji / "thanks"). A real back-and-forth exists.
Qualified TPB prospectLead in a two-way convo who has a problem TPB solves, can pay $97/mo, and has shown buying intent language. Ready for the TPB offer.
Qualified Academy/B.O.S.S. prospectMeets the Academy Qualification Matrix: operating breeder with revenue/ambition to justify a high-ticket build, budget signal, decision-maker, real timeline. Ready for a call.
Qualified appointmentA booked call with a qualified Academy prospect, on the calendar, with reminders set. Booked ≠ qualified; both must be true.
Confirmed appointmentProspect re-confirmed within 24h of the call (reply or confirm click).
Completed sales appointmentProspect attended and the call actually happened (showed, stayed, engaged).
Closed saleMoney collected: TPB first payment cleared, or Academy/B.O.S.S. deposit/first payment cleared. Verbal "yes" is not a close.

Routing logic

  • Offer TPB when: real problem, $97 is in reach, wants structure/community now, not ready for or not a fit for a $3K+ build.
  • Route to call when: operating breeder, revenue or clear ambition, budget + decision-maker + timeline, wants done-with/for-you help.
  • Nurture when: good fit, wrong timing. Tag + dated next action. Not a disqualify.
  • Disqualify when: no breeding intent, can't pay, competitor, abusive, or just browsing. Tag + close. Protect the calendar.

Anti-gaming guardrails (stops KPI inflation)

  • Appointment KPIs pay/credit only on qualified + attended, never on booked. Removes the incentive to book anyone with a pulse.
  • A no-show that was unqualified counts against booking quality, not for booking volume.
  • TPB bounty partially tied to day-30 retention (see Comp). Removes the incentive to hard-sell a churner.
  • Random weekly audit of 5 "qualified" tags by the manager. Mis-tags tracked as a quality metric.

Timeframe answer (the question you asked me not to assume): 100 contacts = DAILY. 20 TPB closes + 20–25 appointments = MONTHLY.
That is the only combination where the numbers reconcile: ~88–100 contacts/day for ~22 days feeds ~20 TPB closes + ~22 appointments at realistic conversion. Daily closes would be absurd (400+/mo); weekly closes would need ~350 contacts/day. Monthly it is.

  • Closed Academy sales are a lagging, shared outcome (needs a capable closer + show rate). Do not make the setter's job security depend on the closer's close rate.

Compensation & Unit Economics

Blunt answer on the proposed package: $200/week base + the bounties is fine for part-time, not for full-time. At 40 hrs/week it is $5/hr base, and even at target total earnings it lands around $12/hr all-in. That is unattractive to a good setter and, if this person is an employee, likely below minimum wage. Either scope it to ~20–25 hrs/week, or raise the base.

Base

Part-time (recommended): $200/wk for ~20–25 hrs = guaranteed floor. If full-time: raise to $350–$400/wk or convert to proper hourly + commission.

TPB bounty

$20 / TPB close, split $12 on first payment cleared + $8 at day-30 still active. ~20.6% of first month; the day-30 tail kills the churn incentive.

Academy / B.O.S.S. setter bounty

Tiered by cash collected on the deal the setter sourced + booked + that attended. Not a percentage, a bounty, so no one argues what "%" meant.

09

First 30 / 60 / 90 Days

Learning and pipeline-building first, full production later. Do not hold a new setter to mature-pipeline close targets in month one, there is no mature pipeline yet.

Days 1–30 · Learn

  • Product, ICP, offers, scripts, CRM
  • Role-play TPB offer + qualification
  • Shadow/observe real conversations
  • Build pipeline, don't chase closes
  • Volume at 50–60% of target
  • Milestone: first TPB closes (3–6), clean CRM, rates being captured

Days 31–60 · Develop

  • Volume to ~80% of target
  • Own warm inbound fully
  • First qualified appointments set
  • Weekly coaching on the leak points
  • Milestone: 8–12 TPB + 8–14 appointments, measured funnel rates exist

Days 61–90 · Produce

  • Full target volume + quality
  • Consistent qualified appointments
  • Graduation: hitting target scorecard 2 weeks running
  • Then decide: continue as setter, or begin closer apprenticeship
Ramped targets beat flat targets. Month 1 target 3–6 TPB. Month 2: 8–12. Month 3+: 16 (the mature target). Judging a day-10 setter against a 16-close month is how you lose a good hire who was actually on track.

Accountability & Management

Review cadence

  • Daily: leading-indicator scorecard + CRM hygiene (self-logged, manager glances)
  • Weekly: full funnel rates, show rate, closes, 2 conversation reviews, 1 coaching focus
  • Monthly: scorecard vs target, comp reconciliation, ramp/role decision

Ownership

  • Coaching + performance: sales manager (or founder until that seat exists)
  • Lead volume: Marketing. A dry funnel is not a setter failure.
  • Close rate on calls: closer. A weak closer is not a setter failure.

Legal & Compensation Risk

Not legal advice. Have qualified employment counsel in your state review all of this before you hire. These are the flags, not clearances.
RiskWhy it matters here
Minimum wage / overtime (FLSA)If this is an employee, $200/wk for full-time is ~$5/hr, below the $7.25 federal floor and far below most state minimums ($15+ in many). Overtime applies past 40 hrs. Commission does not erase the hourly-minimum obligation each pay period.
Worker classificationA set schedule, company scripts/CRM, supervision, coaching, and a prescriptive scorecard all point toward employee under the IRS factors, the DOL economic-reality test, and especially California's ABC test (AB5). Calling someone a "contractor" does not make them one.
Written commission agreementSeveral states require commission terms in writing (e.g., California Labor Code §2751). Define: what is earned, when, how computed, clawbacks, and post-termination.
Earned commission = wagesIn many states, once earned, commission is a protected wage you cannot retroactively forfeit. This drives the "cash cleared before last day pays out" rule.
Chargeback / clawback termsDeducting a clawback from future pay can itself run into wage-deduction rules. Counsel should word this.
Platform ToSNot a law, but Instagram automation / mass DM violates ToS and risks the account. Treat as a hard operational rule.
Cleanest path: a part-time W-2 employee at a lawful hourly base + the commission plan, with a written commission agreement, is simpler and lower-risk than trying to make this prescriptive role fit a 1099. Confirm with counsel.

Final Recommended Operating Model

My strongest recommendation, not a menu: Hire one part-time setter (~25 hrs/week), W-2, at $200/week base + $20/TPB (split for retention) + the banded Academy bounty. Run a 60-day measured pilot with ramped targets. The job is TPB closing + Academy appointment-setting only. Prove the funnel rates and TPB retention on real data, then decide whether to go full-time, add a closer apprenticeship, or hire a second setter. Do not scale on benchmarks.

H. Top 10 blind spots you are likely overlooking

  1. Lead volume is the real ceiling. A great setter against a dry top-of-funnel still fails. Marketing must feed this.
  2. The closer is a hidden dependency. Setting great appointments is worthless if attended→close is weak. Measure the closer too.
  3. TPB churn silently breaks the economics. 16 closes that churn by month 2 is not growth. The day-30 bounty tail is deliberate.
  4. $200/wk full-time is likely illegal and will churn your setter. Scope part-time or raise base.
  5. Classification. This role looks like an employee. A 1099 label will not hold up.
  6. Instagram can ban the account and erase the pipeline overnight. Volume caps are protection, not timidity.
  7. Attribution fights will happen the first time a TPB member ascends. Write the rules before, not after.
  8. You have no measured conversion data. Every projection is a benchmark. The pilot's job is to replace them.
  9. Offer instability. B.O.S.S. is $3K here, $6K on the live page. A moving price breaks qualification and comp. Freeze it.
  10. Founder-dependency. Without a defined scorecard + weekly cadence + CRM-as-truth, this role quietly becomes another thing you manage daily.

I. The 5 decisions to make before hiring

  1. Part-time or full-time? (Recommend part-time. If full-time, raise base.)
  2. Employee or contractor? (Recommend part-time W-2; confirm with counsel.)
  3. Canonical B.O.S.S. price? $3K or $6K. Freeze it before writing comp.
  4. Who coaches + reviews weekly? You, until a sales manager exists. Name it.
  5. Is Marketing committed to the lead volume? ~1,900 contactable prospects/month of raw material. If not, lower the targets now.

J. Is this role ready to hire now?

Yes to hire. No to the plan as written.

Hire the seat, it is the right role and the economics work. But do not sign the exact package in your brief. Before the offer goes out, make the 5 decisions above, freeze the B.O.S.S. price, re-label the 20-close/100-contact targets as monthly/daily stretch goals with a ramp, scope it part-time (or raise the base), and get a written, counsel-reviewed commission agreement. Do that and this is a profitable, low-supervision seat. Skip it and you get a misclassified, underpaid setter chasing a baseline that is mathematically a stretch, and you will churn them in a quarter.
