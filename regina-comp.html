The path I'd actually create

1
Prove the role
First 30–60 days

$200/week base + current commissions. Success = dependable calling coverage, qualified appointments, accurate CRM records, and economically valuable sales opportunities. Do not promise a particular income during this phase.

2
Become a proven pipeline producer
Potentially months 2–6

Expand toward 20–25 authorized hours/week with a corresponding increase in guaranteed compensation — subject to lead demand and cash flow. Keep the attended-appointment and collected-revenue incentives. The promotion trigger is sustained performance over several weeks, not time in the seat.

3
Expand into a higher-value sales role
Only when company economics support it

To make $80K realistic, Regina would likely need to become a full-time sales producer / closer / pipeline manager with real revenue responsibility — higher guaranteed comp and commissions matched to expanded responsibility, not simply more hours making calls.

The one strategic question

Do you want Regina to stay an appointment setter, or eventually become a high-ticket closer? It decides whether $80K belongs in the budget.

If she stays booking

Don't build the company budget around an $80K earning expectation for her. The volume required (~$1.6M originated) isn't a sensible base case for a part-time setter.

If she becomes a closer

There's a far more direct economic path to $80K — a real closing role with salary + 7% on attributable revenue — once the sales operation can feed her enough qualified opportunities.
