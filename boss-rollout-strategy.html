The Rollout & Positioning Strategy

How to make BOSS feel like the natural evolution of what BreedWise learned through Academy, without implying Academy was incomplete, and without making Accelerator look overpriced.

The strategic sentence · the north star

BOSS is not a replacement for Academy and it is not a cheaper Accelerator. It is BreedWise's standardized implementation offer: built from what Academy taught us, designed to turn diagnosed priorities into operating systems, and intentionally bounded so Accelerator remains the home for bespoke acceleration.

If every product, sales, delivery and community decision agrees with that sentence, the architecture stays clean. Optimize the Academy rollout for trust first, conversion second. Counterintuitively, that also maximizes $3K conversions: make it unmistakable that nobody has to buy BOSS to keep what they already paid for, then make the implementation advantage so tangible that the right members want to upgrade.

2

Separate BOSS Systems from BOSS Implementation

Probably the most important decision. Let Academy members access BOSS Systems as they are released (Buyer, Profit, Operational, Breeding, Sales, etc.). That feels like BreedWise investing in existing members. But accessing the systems is not the same thing as buying BOSS.

Academy Legacy Access
Learn it.

The applicable BOSS education, frameworks, standards, tools and templates, within their existing Academy experience.

BOSS — $3K
Build it with us.

A defined engagement: Assess → Prioritize → Build → Implement → Measure → Reassess, with a personalized plan, systems configured/built with them, implementation management, adoption, KPIs and a Definition of Done.

You already have access to the thinking. The upgrade is for members who want us involved in putting it into operation.

Now nobody feels like the good information is being held hostage to extract another $3K.

3

Protect Accelerator even more aggressively

Tell existing Accelerator members: “BOSS is becoming BreedWise's standardized operating system. Where those systems are relevant to your Accelerator work, you benefit from them too. Accelerator remains our higher-touch environment for businesses that require more customization, multiple priorities, faster iteration and deeper strategic involvement.” Don't make them buy BOSS, and don't let BOSS gradually absorb every custom thing you've ever done in Accelerator.

BOSS

Standardized systems, personalized diagnosis and implementation.

Accelerator

Bespoke strategy, multiple constraints, custom growth projects, greater founder access, faster iteration.

A $3K BOSS client should never accidentally receive the same experience as a ~$20K Accelerator client just because the team wants to be helpful. That is one of the biggest potential blind spots.

5

Do not publicly launch BOSS yet

Run controlled implementation with existing members first. Don't position it as “Paid-in-full Academy members get BOSS” (that's an internal selection criterion if you want one). Externally, call it the BOSS Founding Implementation Cohort. Select ~5–8 members on readiness:

  • They respond quickly and have a clear problem.
  • They'll provide the information you need, and will actually implement.
  • They represent different types of constraints.
  • They'll give feedback and can become good case studies.

If you comp or subsidize those first implementations, document why: pilot participation in exchange for feedback, implementation participation, measurement and case-study permission. So when someone later asks “why did Sarah get this and I'm asked to pay $3K?” the answer is not “because she paid Academy off first.”

6

What to learn from the pilot

Don't use the pilot to decide whether people like BOSS. You're testing the machine. By the end, Jennifer should be able to answer:

Question
How long does onboarding take? How long does diagnosis take?
Average Jenn time per client per week? Average Austin time per client per month?
What does AI actually save? Which systems repeat across clients?
Which requests create scope creep? Where do clients get stuck?
What information delays implementation? What should be templated next?
What should BOSS explicitly NOT include?
How many simultaneous clients can Jenn actually manage? What does successful Day 120 look like?

Every time you do something custom, ask: Was this truly custom? If it happens twice: Can we standardize it? Then: Template → SOP → AI Workflow → BOSS Standard. That's how BOSS becomes more profitable as it grows, not less.

7

What MUST be finished before the $3K launch

You don't need a giant finished curriculum. You need the delivery machine finished. Don't launch publicly until these are clear:

  • The promise. One sentence on what happens over the implementation period.
  • Scope. Exactly what $3K does and doesn't include.
  • The BOSS Loop. Assess → Prioritize → Build → Implement → Measure → Reassess.
  • Onboarding. Payment → agreement → intake → baseline → kickoff → plan.
  • Implementation Plan. Every client: Primary Constraint → Objective → Systems → KPIs → Definition of Done.
  • Ownership. Who owns every handoff: Austin, Jenn, Yasmin, Emmanuel, client.
  • Client dashboard. Every active account: status, current priority, next action, owner, due date, health.
  • Support boundaries. Where they communicate, response times, what qualifies for escalation.
  • Austin boundaries. Exactly when and how much Austin is involved.
  • System scope. Especially website, CRM, automation, anything that can become an endless custom project.
  • Capacity ceiling. Max active implementations before sales are throttled.
  • Agreement / payment terms.
  • Academy positioning. Accelerator positioning.
  • Day-120 exit. What happens when implementation ends.

If those aren't defined, volume will expose every ambiguity simultaneously.

8

What is completely OK to leave unfinished

Prevent perfectionism. You do NOT need:

  • Every future BOSS course recorded, or every conceivable tool or automation.
  • Every system someone might eventually need.
  • BOSS Certification completely built, or a giant case-study library.
  • Every SOP perfected, or the entire 12-month continuation experience produced.
  • A solution for every breeder problem.

You do need to know what you're selling, how you diagnose, how you decide what gets implemented, who implements it, how you measure it, and when you're done. That's enough to launch.

9

The volume issue is bigger than the sales issue

At $3K, BOSS may be far easier to sell than Accelerator. That's great until you sell 10 in one week. A 120-day engagement means every new weekly cohort stacks on the previous cohorts. Steady-state active 120-day implementations:

~34
at 2 starts / week
~85
at 5 starts / week
~170
at 10 starts / week
The initial cap

Cap the team at ~2 new BOSS starts per week. Not two sales. Two starts. Sell future start dates if needed.

After 30–45 days, Jennifer can say: “We're at 28 active, 25 green, average implementation time is X, my workload is Y, Austin is spending Z. We can safely move to three starts/week.” Scale based on how many people Jennifer can get to implementation without quality dropping, not on how many Regina can close.

11

How to unveil this to Academy

Don't start with pricing. Do an Academy-only unveiling first. The message:

BOSS wasn't created from scratch. A lot of it was born from what we've learned inside Academy. Academy showed us how important assessment, prioritization, implementation and accountability are. We've gotten much clearer on how to turn those ideas into actual operating systems. That's what BOSS is. And because you helped us get here as Academy members, we're rolling BOSS systems into your experience as Legacy Access.

Academy isn't being renamed BOSS, your existing experience isn't being taken away, and you don't need to purchase anything else. Then teach the BOSS philosophy, let them experience some of the new systems, and release the implementation opportunity separately. That prevents the unveiling from feeling like a webinar designed to extract another $3K.

12

Then make the $3K invitation

A separate module/call: “Want us to help you implement it?” Distinguish clearly:

BOSS Systems Legacy Access

Included for qualifying/current Academy members, per the transition policy you establish.

BOSS Implementation — $3,000

Our team diagnoses the priority, builds the applicable infrastructure, puts it into operation, and measures it.

The message isn't
“Get more BOSS.”
It's
“You've seen the system. If you'd rather have our team work with you to diagnose the priority, build the applicable infrastructure, put it into operation and measure it, that's what the BOSS Implementation engagement is for.”

That converts better because the difference is obvious.

13

Don't manufacture urgency

If you give Academy a founding/legacy opportunity, make the constraint real.

Legitimate scarcity
“We can take eight implementations in this cohort, because this requires hands-on work from our team.”
Not this
“$3K price disappears Friday!!!”

If 20 people want it, the next 12 receive start dates or join the implementation queue. That protects delivery and creates legitimate demand.

15

Your biggest blind spots

Scope creep

At $3K, “we'll help implement your systems” can accidentally become a $20K custom consulting engagement.

Austin creep

Every unusual client problem escalates to you. Suddenly a $3K product consumes more founder time than Accelerator.

Perceived replacement

If Academy hears “finally we'll help you implement,” they'll reasonably ask what they already paid for. Every message must honor Academy as the foundation BOSS grew from.

Accelerator compression

If BOSS includes websites, CRM, strategy, implementation, private support, custom builds, Austin access and multiple priorities for $3K, the rational customer asks why Accelerator costs more. Keep the boundary hard.

+ Giving too much away in the pilot

Founding Academy members can receive extraordinary treatment while you learn. Just don't turn every pilot exception into the public BOSS promise. Document BOSS STANDARD vs. PILOT EXCEPTION.

Part II · The verification layer

BOSS™ Certification Architecture

Make certification its own workstream before the BOSS rollout. It is one of the easiest places to accidentally diminish Academy or make a promise you cannot operationally support.

The governing principle

BOSS can help a member build toward the standard. Certification verifies that the standard has actually been met.

So buying BOSS does not buy certification, and existing Academy members do not lose the certification opportunity they already purchased.

Grandfather the promise, not an automatic pass.

If Academy members were promised an opportunity to become certified, honor that opportunity. That does not mean everyone automatically receives certification because the product is changing. They still have to demonstrate whatever standards BreedWise ultimately requires.

C2

What BOSS Certification must (and must not) mean

It must NOT mean
“BreedWise guarantees this breeder produces healthy puppies.”
“This breeding is medically appropriate.”
“This breeder will never lose puppies.”
“BreedWise's veterinarian approves every decision this breeder makes.”
It means
BreedWise certification verifies that a breeder has demonstrated the required knowledge, documented practices, systems, preparedness and BreedWise standards at the time of audit. That is defensible and measurable.

Build certification around four gates:

Gate 1
Knowledge

Do they understand the principles they are expected to understand? Reproduction, genetics and health, whelping, neonatal preparedness, business practices, ethics.

Gate 2
Verification

Can they provide the required documentation? Health testing, registration where applicable, records, veterinary relationship, contracts and policies, program documentation, other BreedWise requirements. The scientific and health requirements are reviewed and approved by Emmanuel / veterinary leadership before publishing.

Gate 3
Implementation

Are the required practices and systems actually being used? Not “did you watch the training?” but “show us how this operates inside your program.” This is where BOSS becomes extremely valuable.

Gate 4
Audit

BreedWise verifies the evidence against a defined standard.

The result is one of three:

CERTIFIED · standard demonstrated CONDITIONAL / CORRECTIONS REQUIRED · specific gaps NOT YET · material requirements not demonstrated

Use “Not Yet” in member communications rather than “Failed.”

C4

What to tell current Academy members

This is the politically sensitive part. When BOSS launches, tell them explicitly:

Say this, in these words

Your existing Academy certification opportunity is not being taken away or replaced. As BreedWise develops the BOSS standards, we will map the qualifying work you have already completed in Academy toward the applicable certification requirements. You will not be required to purchase BOSS simply to preserve the certification opportunity included with your Academy membership. If you choose to upgrade to BOSS Implementation, our team can work more directly with you to implement applicable systems and close identified gaps, but purchasing BOSS does not guarantee certification.

That eliminates the interpretation you must never create: “I paid Academy expecting certification, and now you are charging me another $3K to get it.”

C5

Existing work counts

Do not make existing members start over. If a member already completed health-testing verification, education requirements, assessment work, documents, or implementation work that satisfies the new standard, credit it. A certification dashboard should read like this:

C6

For new BOSS customers: separate the 120-day promise from certification

120 Days to Build

Assess → Prioritize → Build → Implement → Measure → Reassess. At Day 120 they receive a BOSS Implementation Report: what was identified, built, implemented, what is being measured, what evidence exists, what remains incomplete, and the next roadmap. They are not automatically certified.

12 Months to Prove

After at least 12 months of qualifying participation and evidence, a member becomes eligible for the complete certification audit. The 120 BOSS days count toward that period. 12 months = eligible, not automatically certified.

The tagline

120 Days to Build. 12 Months to Prove.

This is also what makes continuation make obvious sense. The full ladder:

TPB Diagnose it. Know what needs improvement.
↓
BOSS Build it. Put the systems into operation.
↓
BOSS Continuation Operate + prove it. Measure, improve, establish evidence.
↓
Certification Verify it. Independent verification against the standard.
↓
Accelerator Accelerate it. Bespoke growth beyond the standard.

Certification is not something to awkwardly sell. It becomes a standard members work toward.

C7

One important commercial decision

Legacy protection

Do not make $297 continuation mandatory solely to maintain certification eligibility for legacy Academy members, unless their original agreement clearly allows it.

For new customers, structure the future certification pathway prospectively however you want, with counsel reviewing the representations and terms. For existing Academy members, preserve what they were promised. That is why two paths should temporarily coexist:

Path A
Legacy Academy Certification

Honors the original promise. Qualifying Academy work maps forward.

Path B
New BOSS Certification

The forward standard for new customers, defined prospectively with counsel.

Eventually the legacy cohort disappears naturally.

C8

What must be finished before announcing certification

This is one area not to launch half-built. Before publicly explaining BOSS Certification, these must exist:

  • Certification Standard. Exactly what must be true.
  • Evidence Matrix. What proves each requirement.
  • Reviewer. Who can approve each requirement.
  • Scientific Governance. Which standards require Emmanuel / veterinary approval.
  • Audit Process. How certification decisions happen.
  • Legacy Mapping. How Academy requirements map into the new requirements.
  • Status System. Certified / Corrections Required / Not Yet.
  • Appeal / Re-review Process. What happens when someone disagrees.
  • Expiration / Renewal. Whether certification expires and what continuing verification looks like.
  • Public Claims Policy. Exactly what members may say certification means.
  • Badge / Brand Usage. How someone may use “BreedWise Certified.”
  • Revocation Policy. What happens if a certified breeder later materially violates the standard.
The rule

The standard must exist before the badge exists.

Anything involving reproduction, genetics, health, neonatal practices or veterinary preparedness: Emmanuel and the appropriate veterinary / scientific reviewers sign off on the actual standards before publishing.

What can remain unfinished:

  • Every certification lesson recorded.
  • Fancy certification software or a beautiful automated portal.
  • Every audit automated.
  • The final public badge, immediately. First audits can run manually.

But the standard and evidence requirements must be finalized before telling members what certification represents.

C9

Blind spot: certification can become a fulfillment monster

If every certification requires Austin plus Jennifer plus Emmanuel to manually inspect 80 documents and spend three hours on Zoom, it will not scale. While designing it, every requirement must answer:

  • What are we verifying?
  • What evidence proves it?
  • Can Jennifer verify it?
  • Does this actually require a veterinarian?
  • Can the evidence be standardized?

Emmanuel should not become the bottleneck for administrative verification. Use him for the things that legitimately require scientific or veterinary judgment. Ownership splits cleanly:

Jennifer

Owns the certification process.

Emmanuel / Vet

Owns the scientific standard and escalated scientific review.

Austin

Owns the business and brand standard and final architecture, not every audit.

The clean message for the entire rollout

Academy helped members learn, assess and improve. BOSS turns those lessons into a defined operating system and helps members put the right systems into operation. Certification does not mean you bought BOSS, it means you have demonstrated the BreedWise standard. And Accelerator remains the environment for breeders who need more customized strategy and acceleration beyond the standardized BOSS system.
