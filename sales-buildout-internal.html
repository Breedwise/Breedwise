01

Compensation Design Decisions

Set the money language straight first: do not call the Academy setter payout "5 to 10%." Your examples ($150 on $1,500 collected, $300 on $6,000+) are a tiered bounty. Make it explicit so nobody argues later about what a percentage meant.

Why a $20 TPB bounty

If Academy pays commission and TPB does not, Regina will naturally over-qualify people for Academy. The $20 rewards the correct recommendation, not the most expensive one. At $97/mo it is ~20.6% of first-month payment; no recurring TPB commission.

Pay on cash, not calendar

Do not pay for simply booking a call; that incentivizes putting anyone with a pulse on your calendar. The setter tier pays on collected cash, so variable pay is tied to quality. Comparable DM-setter roles advertise 2–5% of closed deals. DynamisRev

Closer comp — your rules

  • Start at 10% of net cash collected on deals she personally closes, paid as cash is collected (e.g. $150 now on a $1,500 deposit toward $6,000, then $450 as the balance comes in). Dial A Closer
  • Do NOT stack the $300 setter commission + the 10% closer commission when she sets and closes her own deal. One or the other.
  • 12.5% for self-sourced-and-closed is a later move, not now. Company-set lead she closes stays 10%. Let her prove she can close first.
02

Pilot Economics & Workload

$200/week base for the pilot (~$867/month). Keeps you below the $250 target while guaranteeing pay for consistently working the pipeline. But define the workload, or $200/week quietly becomes "available seven days a week all day."

Coverage

Mon–Fri primary · weekend light/assigned only · 2–3 defined inbox checks daily, not "always online."

Track actual hours

During the pilot, so you know whether $200/week is sustainable before you lock it in.

Lead volume is yours

She cannot earn $7K if the machine does not create conversations. That is Marketing's job, not Regina's.

03

Classification & Legal

Worker classification: if Regina is treated as an employee rather than a genuine contractor, have payroll or counsel review the base-plus-commission arrangement, hours, minimum wage / overtime and classification. Calling someone a contractor does not determine status; the economic reality does. U.S. Dept. of Labor
Put the entire compensation plan in writing before relying on it. Commission terms can constitute promised wages depending on status and jurisdiction, and changing or recovering earned commissions can carry wage-law consequences.
04

The $6.5–7K Math (Austin's view)

She probably cannot replace a $6,500–$7,000/month job as only a setter under these economics. At max $300 setter payout: $6,500 − ~$867 base = $5,633 in commission = about 19 Academy closes a month. For $7,000, about 21. That is a closer's output, not a setter's.

06

Commission Rules to Define in Writing

  • What counts as Regina-sourced?
  • What counts as a TPB conversion?
  • What counts as Academy setter credit?
  • Books with Regina, closes 30 days later?
  • No-shows, then you later close them?
  • Jennifer later ascends a TPB member Regina first spoke with?
  • What is "Net Cash Collected"? Taxes excluded?
  • Refunds? Chargebacks?
  • When are commissions paid?
  • Can two people get attribution?
  • Setter stacks with closer? No.

08

Day-30 Readiness Evaluation

Decide on evidence, not on whether you "feel" she's ready. At day 30, choose: continue as Setter, or advance to Setter + Closer Apprentice.

Evaluate:

  • Conversation quality
  • Qualification accuracy
  • Booked-call quality
  • Show rate
  • Downstream close rate
  • TPB conversions
  • Revenue generated
  • Follow-up discipline
  • CRM discipline
  • Coachability
09

How to Present It to Regina

Don't say "I'm giving you a $200/week Instagram job." $200/week isn't the opportunity. The opportunity is that BreedWise pays her to learn the front of the sales process, pays commissions while she learns, then develops her into a 10%-of-collected-cash closer. Lead with the career, show the math, never guarantee the income.
