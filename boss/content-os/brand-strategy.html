Brand Strategy

Branding is what you’re constantly associated with. Your job is to make sure you’re always associated with the right things.
Frame the module. Branding is what you constantly associate with, and we want to be associated with the right things. A lot of breeders focus on being overly competitive, stroking their ego, or being the biggest and baddest — but those things are not what make people want to buy from you. This module builds the brand on purpose.
What branding really is

Branding is the associations you make

In today’s era, branding is the associations you constantly make with the things you want to be known for.
Your breedEducationQuality TransparencyYour familyReal resultsConsistency
Your brand is the sum of what people think when they hear your name.
Branding is essentially the associations you make, constantly, with the things you want people to know you for. Every post, every caption, every photo is depositing an association. The question is always: is this adding the association I want to be known for?

I summarize a brand in what I call brand pillars — the details of your brand that you commit to, day in and day out. To stay consistent without overcomplicating things, you want exactly four. Four pillars hold the whole brand up.

Pillar 04

Your difference is the edge

The thing only you have. Pick what’s true to you and commit to it.

The fourth pillar is whatever makes you different — your culture, the purpose of your program, positivity, your family dynamic, your unique personality, the relationship you have with your dogs. You don’t need all of them; pick the ones that are genuinely you and make them unmistakable.

A successful brand stands on these four.
For a successful brand you need to know the purpose of your breed, the purpose of your program, who you’re called to serve — your clients, your ICP — and the specific segment of the market you’re focused on, your niche. Pillars are what people see; this is the foundation underneath them.
ICP & niche

Serve a niche, not everyone

  • Most breeders just produce puppies hoping it clicks.
  • Narrow from the whole market to your niche — the segment you focus on.
  • Then to your ICP — the exact client you’re called to serve.
A lot of breeders are strictly focused on producing puppies from a specific breed, hoping it clicks — but there’s more to it. Know who you’re called to serve, your ICP, and the specific segment of the market you’re focused on, your niche. The tighter the center, the stronger the pull.
Positioning

What type of brand are you?

The Educator
  • Teaches the breed
  • Builds authority and trust
  • Grows through value
Known for what you teach
The Premium
  • Sells the experience
  • Commands higher prices
  • Grows through desire
Known for how you make them feel
You also have to decide what type of brand you’re going to create. Are you more of an educator — leading with teaching and authority? Or more of a premium brand — leading with experience and desire? Both work. Pick the lane that fits you, and commit, because it shapes every piece of content.
Consistency

Then execute it premium

The premium brand kit
  • A premium logo
  • A premium bio
  • A premium mission statement
  • A consistent content format
  • Consistent brand pillars
Whatever type you choose, execute it at a premium level: a premium logo, a premium bio, a premium mission statement, and a consistent content format and set of pillars. Consistency across these is what makes the brand feel official instead of accidental.
The big idea

Be known on purpose

Associate with the right things. Commit to four pillars. Build on breed purpose, program purpose, your ICP, and your niche. Choose your type, and execute it premium — every day.
