This Isn’t Breeding 101

You’ve Already Built a Breeding Program. Now Build the Systems Behind It.

You already know what it takes to produce a litter. You’ve paid stud fees. Run progesterone. Lost sleep during whelping. Answered endless puppy inquiries. Spent thousands on your dogs. Made placements. Handled unexpected expenses. Learned lessons the expensive way.

The question now isn’t whether you can produce puppies. It’s whether the program behind those puppies is operating like a professional business.

Do you know, without guessing:

  • What your last litter actually cost?
  • What you made after every real expense?
  • Why your puppies are priced where they are?
  • How to communicate enough value to defend that price?
  • Where qualified buyers for your next litter are coming from?
  • What happens from inquiry to qualification to deposit?
  • Whether your next whelp is supported by a repeatable preparation system?
  • What your biggest operational weakness is over the next 12 months?

If several of those answers aren’t immediately clear, that is exactly what these two days are designed to fix.

Revenue Is Not Profit

Selling the Litter Doesn’t Automatically Mean the Litter Was Profitable.

Money coming in feels like success. But revenue and profit are not the same thing, and most programs have never worked out the difference on their own numbers. Every litter carries real costs:

Stud & service feesProgesterone & reproductive careTravel & shippingHealth testingVeterinary careC-section & emergency careWhelping suppliesFoodRegistrationMarketingYour time & laborPuppy careUnexpected complications

If you’re pricing primarily by looking at what other breeders charge, you may have no idea whether your current pricing actually supports your standards.

And profitability isn’t only a pricing problem. If puppies arrive before demand exists, your leverage changes. Now you’re posting harder, answering more messages, entertaining lower-quality inquiries, hearing “what’s your lowest?”, watching puppies get older, and questioning whether you should discount.

That is not a marketing problem in isolation. It’s a system problem.

The Intensive connects your economics, pricing, demand, buyer process and veterinary preparation into one breeding program strategy.

The Cost of Guessing

Another Litter Is an Expensive Place to Keep Guessing.

You do not need dozens of additional litters to discover what works through trial and error. You need to identify the gaps before the next litter exposes them.

A pricing mistake can follow every puppy in a litter. A demand problem becomes obvious when puppies are already on the ground. A preparation gap often becomes visible when the pressure is highest.

The goal of this Intensive is to find those gaps before they become another expensive lesson.

Questions, Answered

Who is this for?

Established breeders already producing litters, generally around 2–3+ per year, who want stronger economics, pricing, demand, buyer systems and veterinary-backed preparation. This is not designed as a first-litter course.

I’ve been breeding for years. Will this be too basic?

No. Breeders with 7–13 years of experience have reported meaningful changes to how they price, sell and prepare after working with BreedWise. The Intensive assumes breeding experience and focuses on the business and preparation systems many experienced breeders never formally built.

Does this teach me how to breed dogs?

No. This is not Breeding 101. It works on the business behind your program (economics, pricing, demand, buyer process) and veterinary-backed preparation systems (dam, whelping, neonatal), not the fundamentals of how to breed.

Can this actually help me make more money?

We don’t guarantee outcomes. The Intensive helps you understand your economics, pricing, positioning, demand and sales, all of which influence business performance. Results depend on your market, program, breed, pricing and execution.

Why does veterinary education belong in a profitability event?

Because profitability without standards is not the BreedWise philosophy. A professional breeding program needs both sustainable economics and systems designed around responsible care. That is the whole point of pairing Austin with Dr. Fontaine.

Is Dr. Fontaine replacing my veterinarian?

Absolutely not. His material is educational frameworks only, designed to help you prepare better and collaborate more effectively with your own veterinarian.

What if my breed has different pricing?

This isn’t about imposing a universal puppy price. It’s about understanding your own economics, market, positioning and value, so your pricing fits your program rather than someone else’s.

What if I’m not good at sales?

You don’t need to become a pushy salesperson. The focus is qualification, communicating value, structured follow-up, and helping the right buyer make a confident decision.

Do I need a large social media following?

No. The demand system is not built to depend entirely on follower count. It’s about creating and converting qualified interest before your next litter needs homes.

What happens if I miss part of the live event?

General Admission includes 14-day replay access, and VIP includes lifetime recordings. Dogs don’t care about your calendar, so we built in a cushion.

What’s the difference between GA and VIP?

General Admission gives you the system, frameworks and working event. VIP adds additional personalized implementation support and expert review of your own program, business and breeding audits, implementation labs, a private plan review, and lifetime recordings.

What do I actually leave with?

Your completed litter economics, a pricing framework and value stack, a 90-Day Next-Litter Demand Plan, a clearer buyer process, a whelping & neonatal preparation checklist, and a 12-Month Roadmap with 30-day priorities, all built during the event.

What happens after the Intensive?

You leave with a plan you can run on your own. For breeders who want ongoing coaching and accountability, BreedWise Academy is an optional next step, not a requirement.
