The Closing Call Framework

The consultative close for the done-with-you 120-day build. Diagnose the gap between knowing and implementing, confirm the one priority that matters, and make the decision easy for the breeders it genuinely fits.

DRAFT · confirm upgrade pricing + agreement language with counsel before quoting
Operating principle

We don't pressure breeders into BOSS. We diagnose whether BOSS is right, then make it simple for the ones it fits. A clean "not right now" is a good outcome that protects the brand and their Academy relationship.

Pre-call prep

5 min before

Objective: walk in with a point of view, not a blank page. You should be able to guess their number-one constraint before they say a word.

Pull up

  • Their Program Assessment / scorecard results The lowest-scoring area is your likely wedge.
  • Which lane they're in Existing Academy member (upgrade at reduced price) vs brand-new lead ($6,000).
  • Known numbers Litters per year, average price, where their leads come from, how long they've been breeding.
  • Skool / engagement activity What have they watched, asked, or implemented already?
Write one sentence before the call: "My best guess at their #1 constraint is ______." You'll confirm or correct it in diagnosis, but you enter with a hypothesis.
1

Frame the call

First 3 min

Objective: set the agenda, give them permission to say no, and lower the pressure so they'll be honest with you.

SayHere's how I'd like to use our time. First I want to really understand where your program is right now and where you're trying to take it. Then, if it makes sense for your situation, I'll walk you through exactly how BOSS Implementation would work for you. And if it's not the right fit or the right time, I'll tell you that straight. By the end you'll know either way, and "not right now" is a completely fine place to land. Sound fair?
Get the verbal "yes, fair." That agreement is what earns you honest answers in the next stage. Then stop selling and start listening.
2

Diagnose the gap

15–20 min · the heart of the call

Objective: get them to say out loud that the problem isn't knowing what to do, it's that it hasn't been built and isn't running consistently. You are mapping the distance between knowing and implementing.

Current state

  • "Walk me through your program today. How many litters a year, and what's actually working well?"
  • "When a really good inquiry comes in, what happens next, step by step?" Listen for: no system, lives in their head, follow-up is inconsistent.
  • "How do you track your litter economics right now?" Listen for: they don't, or they guess.
  • "What's the one thing you know you should have fixed by now but haven't?"

Cost of the constraint (quantify it)

  • "If that follow-up were tight, how many more of the right buyers would you keep in a year? What's one placement worth to you?"
  • "What has not having this cost you over the last 12 months, in missed placements, time, or stress?"
  • "On a scale of 1 to 10, how much of your program runs on you remembering to do things?"

Isolate why it hasn't happened yet

  • "You clearly know what to do here. What's actually stopped it from getting built and used consistently?"
The honest answer is almost always: no time, no system, or no one to build it with. That is the BOSS wedge. Don't rescue them from the silence, let them sit with the gap.
Transition line (get agreement before moving on): "So it sounds like the issue isn't that you don't know what to do. It's that it hasn't been built, and it isn't operating consistently. Is that fair?"
3

Confirm the one priority

3 min

Objective: mirror the BOSS Loop live. Assess, then prioritize. Lock the single system that matters most so you can tailor the offer to it.

AskIf we could only fix one system in the next 120 days, the one that would make the biggest difference to your program, which one is it?
Write down their exact words. You will quote them back when you present. BOSS doesn't build everything. It builds what matters next, and you're showing them you operate that way on the call itself.
4

Future pace

3 min

Objective: let them feel the built state before they hear the price. Paint a program that operates, not more information.

Say (tailor to their priority)Picture 120 days from now. A new inquiry hits your site, drops straight into a CRM, and gets the right follow-up without you having to remember it. You can see every buyer in your pipeline. Your litter numbers are entered, and you actually know what each litter produced and kept. That's not more to know. That's a program that runs. That's what we build with you.
5

Present BOSS Implementation

5–8 min

Objective: present only through the lens of their stated constraint. Do not feature-dump. Every element ties back to a word they used.

OpenBased on what you told me, here's exactly how BOSS Implementation would work for your situation.

Walk the four pillars, anchored to their gap

  • PersonalizedBOSS Baseline, a 120-day pathway, priorities and KPIs built around the system they just named.
  • Built + configuredWebsite, CRM and lead-management, workflows and dashboard. Lead hard on whichever directly fixes their constraint.
  • High-touchPrivate implementation channel, dedicated project management, private strategy and expert sessions, reviews at 30/60/90/120.
  • MeasuredKPI tracking, a day-120 reassessment, an Implementation Report, and the next roadmap.
Protect the three-lane clarity so there's zero confusion later: "You keep your Academy no matter what you decide today. This is a separate, optional build where we do it with you. Nothing about your membership changes."
6

Deliver the price

2 min · then silence

Objective: state the number cleanly and confidently, frame it against the cost of inaction, then stop talking.

Say (then be quiet)The full BOSS Implementation build is $6,000. Because you're already an Academy member and you've invested in the education and tools that overlap with BOSS, your upgrade is $3,000.

If they need the frame

You told me a placement is worth about $____ to you, and that you're leaving several on the table every year. The build pays for itself the moment the system starts catching what you're losing right now.
Silence is the tool here. After the number, do not speak first. Let them react.
Team note (DRAFT): confirm the $3,000 upgrade figure and the written agreement are locked before quoting. These are pending counsel review and fulfillment-economics sign-off. Do not improvise discounts or payment terms on the call.
7

Ask for the decision

1 min · then silence

Objective: make a clear recommendation and ask directly. Then stop and let them answer.

Primary closeHonestly, based on everything you've told me, this is clearly the right fit for where your program is. Do you want to get started?
Choice close (alternative)I think the full build makes the most sense for you. Do you want to get it started today, or walk through the kickoff first?
Ask, then stop talking. Whoever speaks first after the close owns the pressure. Let it be them. If yes, go straight to Lock the next step.
8

Objection handling

as needed

Pattern for every objection: acknowledge, isolate ("is that the real thing, or is there something else?"), reframe, then re-close. Never argue.

What they sayHow to handle it
"It's too expensive."Isolate: "Totally fair. Is it the amount itself, or the timing?" Then reframe against the leak they named: "You told me you're losing several placements a year worth $____. This is smaller than the problem it fixes." Re-close.
"I need to talk to my spouse / partner."Respect it: "Of course, this should be a joint decision." Then: "What do you think they'll want to know? Let's make sure you can answer it." Book the follow-up with a specific date, ideally with the partner on the call.
"I can do this myself."Agree, then test it: "You probably can. You know the what. The real question is whether it actually gets built and used in the next 120 days, or sits on the someday list like it has so far. What would be different this time?" DIY is a legitimate lane: Academy + Legacy Access covers it. Don't fight it.
"Let me think about it."Surface the real reason: "Of course. Usually 'let me think' means one of two things: a specific question I didn't answer, or it just doesn't feel like the right time. Which one is it for you?" Then handle the actual objection.
"Will this guarantee more sales / healthy litters?"Hold the line, it builds trust: "I won't promise income or biological outcomes, that wouldn't be honest, and it's not what responsible certification or coaching means. What we guarantee is that we build and implement the systems with you. The results come from running them consistently."
"My Academy term is almost up."Clarify the lanes: nothing auto-converts, and a transition window applies. "Your term ending doesn't force anything. You choose: conclude, upgrade now, or continue later. This upgrade starts a fresh 120-day build from day one."
"Can I just pay monthly / can you discount it?"Do not improvise terms. "Payment options and pricing are set, I can't change them on a call, but let me make sure the value is clear first." Route any exception through the agreed structure, not a verbal promise.
9

Lock the next step

close every call here

Objective: never end vague. Every call ends with a concrete, dated next action.

If they enrolled

  • Take payment / send the agreement on the call while momentum is high.
  • Book the Day-1 BOSS Baseline date right there.
  • Set week-1 expectations and introduce their project manager.
  • Send the content intake link (website-assets portal) so the build can start.

If it's "not right now"

  • Confirm a specific follow-up date and what will have changed by then.
  • Reassure them plainly: "Nothing bad happens. You keep your Academy and your Legacy Access, and the door stays open."
  • Tag them in the CRM with the real reason and the follow-up date for nurture.
Post-call, every time: update the CRM with their named constraint, the priority system they chose, the objection that surfaced, and the next action with a date.
Live-call cheat sheet
FRAME→DIAGNOSE→"not knowing, it's implementing"→PRIORITY→FUTURE PACE→PRESENT to their gap→PRICE + silence→CLOSE + silence→OBJECTION: acknowledge · isolate · reframe · re-close→LOCK next step
  • Diagnose longer than you pitch. The offer sells itself once the gap is clear.
  • Quote them back. Present only through the one priority they named.
  • Silence is a tool. After the price and after the close, do not speak first.
  • Honor Academy always. Nothing is required, nothing auto-converts, a clean no is a win.
  • No guarantees. We guarantee the build, never the biological or income outcome.
