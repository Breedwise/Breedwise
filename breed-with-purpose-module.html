Breed With Purpose

Your breed standard is the blueprint.
The Profitable Breeder · Breed wiser.
Welcome. Short, high-value module — about 15 minutes. If you can, take notes. Today is breed standards: the foundation for breeding success. Set the frame early — it's common for us to pick a dog apart on sight, but actually understanding your breed standard is what lets you improve your program on purpose.
Start here
Do you actually know
your breed standard?
It's easy to look at a dog and pick it apart. It's another thing entirely to know the blueprint you're breeding toward — and most of us were never told to study it.
Intro
Make this personal and honest. Nobody sat most of us down and said "study the standard so you breed for the right reasons — to improve health and reduce flaws." That conversation rarely happens when you first invest in a dog, and it's exactly what sets a serious breeder apart.
The standard is a

Blueprint for your breed

  • A blueprint makes a house buildable — "I think this goes here" does not.
  • It tells you what to aim for — and which flaws to improve.
  • It's your North Star. It never goes out of style.
01 · Standard
Use the house analogy — a blueprint means you build with intention instead of guessing. The standard does two jobs: it shows you the target AND it names the flaws to breed out. And it never goes out of style — clients will always want the breed the way it was meant to be, so the standard is a safe north to steer by.
What a standard tells you

Five things to read for

  • Conformation — the physical blueprint: proportions, head, angulation, color.
  • Flaws — the traits you must not carry forward.
  • Movement & gait — does it move the way the breed should?
  • Temperament — e.g. the bully bred for companionship.
  • Purpose — herd, hunt, or companion. Breed for it.
01 · Standard
Walk the five. Conformation is the measurable physical picture. Flaws matter just as much — if you don't know what you shouldn't have, you can breed inheritable problems right in. Movement, temperament, and purpose round it out: a dog that can't move or has the wrong temperament is going against the standard, not representing it.
The framework

The Breeder
Trifecta

Standard → Vision → Market. In that order.
This is the framework I created to simplify how you put breedings together. Three parts, and the order is everything.
In order
First

Standard

The blueprint. Base every decision — every pairing, every acquisition — on it.

Then

Vision

What you'll fix & improve to get your dogs up to par. Cure your kennel blindness.

Then

Market

The trends. It reacts to quality — so you lead with the standard, not the fad.

02 · Trifecta
Standard first — it's the foundation, so every decision runs through it. Vision is your honest plan to raise your own dogs to the standard. Market comes last on purpose: it's trends, and it will always respond to quality. Decide to the standard, bring your vision, and let the market react.
Most breeders
run it backwards.
  • They start with "what the market wants" — the standard becomes an afterthought.
  • Flip it: decide to the standard, add your vision, and the market adopts you.
  • Because people buy quality — no matter what.
02 · Trifecta
Name the common mistake: chasing the market first and treating the standard as optional. Reference "bring the bully back" — that should have been the goal the whole time. When you breed to the standard with a clear vision, the market comes to you, because quality always wins in the end.
The business case

The standard pays

  • Standard → consistent, predictable results.
  • Results → more people want your dogs (demand).
  • Demand → you can charge premium prices.
  • Premium → a waitlist that fills before the litter does.
Per the AKC, the dogs that best represent the standard carry waitlists of over a year.
03 · Business
This is the money slide. Make the chain explicit: understanding the standard produces consistent results; consistency drives demand; demand lets you raise prices and build a waitlist. And it's not just me saying it — the AKC backs that the dogs repping the standard best command the longest waitlists.
Integrity

Preserve — don't dilute

  • Fads and cash-grabs breed outside the standard.
  • That stacks genetic disorders and dilutes the breed.
  • Breed to preserve: healthier, longer-lived, serving a purpose in someone's life.
03 · Business
We see breeds thrown out of proportion chasing new "styles" — most of it is a cash grab, not an improvement. Over-breeding outside the standard brings genetic disorders and waters the breed down. Your job is preservation: a healthy dog that fulfills a real purpose in a family's life for years.
Breed quality vs pet quality

Not every dog should be bred

  • Be careful which dogs you let carry your lineage.
  • Take your time — be okay pet-homing the wrong pick. A temporary loss buys long-term success.
  • "Not all money is good money." Vet your puppy and stud clients like business partners.
04 · Decisions
Not every dog is meant to be bred, and handing out breeding rights to anyone who pays creates dogs that shouldn't reproduce. Give yourself time to make the right call — it's okay to say "this one's pet quality." And protect your name: a stud or puppy going to the wrong operator reflects on you, so treat them like a client you're choosing to work with.
Health testing

Health is a standard too

  • Test for your breed's known issues — reduce inherited disease.
  • Never line-breed blind — you can stack bad genetics you can't see.
  • Health is the quality a buyer can't see on the outside.
Go deeper with the geneticist (Rob Westra) inside BreedWise.
04 · Decisions
Every breed has traceable, common health issues — test accordingly, and go the extra mile to prove what your dogs don't carry. Line-breeding without testing can quietly stack disease into your lineage and onto the families who buy from you. Point them to the geneticist resources inside the platform to go deeper than this call can.
Cure your kennel blindness.
  • Compare your own dogs to the standard — honestly.
  • Breed to complement a flaw — don't over-exaggerate to fix it.
  • Look for consistency across the lineage, not one lucky litter.
05 · Craft
Kennel blindness is loving your dog past its flaws. Go to the standard and honestly mark where your dog falls short, then choose a mate that corrects it — without swinging to an extreme that introduces new problems. And check the whole line: does the mate come from dogs that consistently don't carry the trait you're trying to remove?
With a definite aim,
you cannot miss.
  • Pair like a puzzle, not a gamble.
  • Think 4, 5, 6 generations — not one litter.
  • Strategic movement beats hoping it works out.
05 · Craft
If you aim at a target you're far likelier to hit it than someone just hoping a pairing works. This is a game of consistency and patience — you're breeding traits out across generations, not rushing one result. Clarity on what you're building makes every decision move you forward instead of sideways.
Get rewarded

Show your receipts

  • People reward what they can see. Nobody pays for work they don't know you did.
  • Document the health tests and the right breedings.
  • Receipts let you charge — and get — premium prices.
05 · Craft
So many breeders do the right things in the right order and never get rewarded, because they don't show it. Document the work — "we health-tested, we made this pairing for this reason." People like receipts; when they can see the proof, they'll pay a premium. Content creation is how the quality becomes visible.
Do this week

Your action plan

  • 1Go to your breed standard and study it — again and again, not once.
  • 2Health test your dogs and keep records of what they do and don't carry.
  • 3Document & showcase the work so you get rewarded for it.
  • 4Run every decision through the Trifecta: Standard → Vision → Market.
Action
Give them the homework clearly. Study the standard — don't do it once and move on. Start (or formalize) health testing and track it. Document the work so the market can reward it. And run every acquisition and pairing through the Trifecta. If they do these four, their program improves on purpose.
The takeaway
Breed with purpose.
Do the right things, in the right order, and the market will adopt you — because people buy quality, no matter what.
