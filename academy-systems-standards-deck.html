Part 1

Systems
vs Standards

Two words that do two different jobs — and you need both.
Quick framing section, about 5 minutes. People use these words interchangeably; separating them is where the clarity comes from.
Two words, two jobs

Standard

The bar. What "good" looks like, written down and non-negotiable. The what.

System

The repeatable process that hits the standard every time, without relying on memory. The how.

01 · Framing
Give a concrete example: a standard is "every puppy leaves vet-checked, microchipped, and with a written care pack." The system is the go-home checklist and the week-by-week process that guarantees it. Standard = the destination; system = the road that gets you there every time.
Why you need both
A standard without a system is a wish.
A system without a standard is busywork.
Together they produce the only thing that scales: predictable excellence.
01 · Framing
Hammer this line. Lots of breeders have high standards they can't consistently hit (a wish), or busy routines that aren't aimed at a clear bar (busywork). The magic is pairing them — a clear standard with a system that delivers it on autopilot.
What's at stake

Standards protect four things

  • The dogs — their health and welfare.
  • The buyers — the families who trust you.
  • The brand — the reputation that fills your waitlist.
  • The price — premium is a standard before it's a number.
01 · Framing
Standards aren't bureaucracy — they're protection. Every standard you set is guarding one of these four. When you frame standards this way, holding them under pressure gets a lot easier because you know what you're protecting.
Part 2

The 5 Core
Operating Systems

Every breeding program, at every size, runs on these five.
This is the heart of the module — about 12 minutes. Five systems. Tell them we'll go one at a time, and for each we'll name the standard, the system, and the one number that tells the truth.
The map

Five systems, one program

  • Demand & Buyers — a waitlist before the litter.
  • Sales & Deposits — qualified buyers, signed and committed.
  • Breeding & Whelping — healthy, planned, veterinary-informed.
  • Puppy-Raising & Go-Home — raised and delivered to a standard.
  • Operations & Numbers — records, pricing, and the truth about profit.
02 · Systems
Give them the whole map first so they see the shape. Then the key line: your program is only as strong as its weakest system. A breeder with great dogs and no demand system starves; a breeder with great demand and no whelping system loses puppies. Balance matters.
System 1

Demand & Buyers

StandardA qualified waitlist that fills before the puppies do — aligned buyers, not whoever shows up.
SystemContent → capture → nurture → application, running whether or not you have a litter on the ground.
MetricQualified inquiries per month · waitlist depth.
02 · Systems
Demand is the system most breeders ignore until they have puppies to place — which is exactly backwards. The standard is a waitlist that fills in advance with the right buyers. The system runs continuously. Scorecards and lead magnets (the Profitable Breeder Scorecard) are the capture mechanism here.
System 2

Sales & Deposits

StandardQualify before you quote. Every placement: a signed contract and a real deposit. No buyer's remorse.
SystemInquiry → qualify call → deposit → contract → buyer onboarding.
MetricInquiry-to-deposit rate.
02 · Systems
Leading with price turns you into a number to compare. The standard is to qualify first and build value, then price with context. A deposit and a contract aren't paperwork — they're how you filter serious buyers and protect both sides. This is also where a setter/booking system plugs in.
System 3 · veterinary-informed

Breeding & Whelping

StandardHealth & genetic testing done and verified. Timing by data, not by guess. A written whelping plan.
SystemPre-breeding checklist → progesterone timing → whelping protocol → newborn scoring & weights.
MetricLive births · survival through 3 weeks.
02 · Systems
This is where the "wise" in BreedWise is earned, and where Dr. Fontaine's work lives. The standard is non-negotiable health and genetics plus data-driven timing. The system turns the riskiest, most emotional part of breeding into a protocol you follow instead of a crisis you react to. Measure births and three-week survival — the numbers that matter most.
System 4

Puppy-Raising & Go-Home

StandardEarly neuro stimulation, real socialization, health-checked, and a go-home experience buyers rave about.
SystemWeek-by-week raising protocol → vet checks → go-home pack & follow-up.
MetricBuyer satisfaction · referral rate.
02 · Systems
The puppy a family takes home is the product — and the experience around it is what generates referrals and premium pricing. The standard covers both the dog (development, health) and the handoff. A week-by-week protocol means nothing gets skipped in the chaos of a litter. Referrals are the metric: delighted buyers fill your next waitlist for free.
System 5

Operations & Numbers

StandardEvery buyer, deposit, and dollar in one place. You know your numbers — before and after every litter.
SystemOne CRM/record system + pricing model + a living SOP library.
MetricProfit per litter · cash collected.
02 · Systems
This is the system that makes it a business instead of a hobby that eats money. One source of truth for contacts and cash — not sticky notes and memory. If you don't know your profit per litter, you're guessing. This system is also where every other system's SOPs get stored.
Recap
Five systems.
One program.
Your program is only as strong as its weakest system. We'll find yours in the workshop.
02 · Systems
Quick breath and recap. Reinforce the weakest-link idea and tease the workshop at the end where they'll score each of the five. Ask them to privately start thinking about which of the five is weakest for them right now.
Part 3

The BreedWise
Standards

The non-negotiable bar your systems are built to hold.
About 7 minutes. These are the standards that define a BreedWise-caliber program. Systems are personal to each breeder; these standards are shared — they're what the name stands for.
First principle
A standard you'll break
under pressure isn't a standard.
  • Money qualifies the tier — never the person.
  • Set the bar where you'll hold it on your hardest day.
03 · Standards
Pressure reveals real standards — a buyer waving cash, a timing shortcut, a health test you'd rather skip. If you'd fold, it was a preference, not a standard. Tie this to the qualification standard: money tells you which program fits, it never makes a bad-fit breeder a good one.
Standard 1 · veterinary-informed

Health & Genetics

  • Test, verify, and breed for health — no shortcuts, no bluffing.
  • Breeding decisions follow the data and veterinary guidance.
  • The dog's welfare outranks the sale, every time.
03 · Standards
This is the standard the whole brand rests on. "I health test" means nothing without verification — what tests, which dogs, which results. Breeding for health sometimes means not breeding a dog you love. That's the standard.
Standard 2

Buyer Experience

  • Qualify, educate, and support — the family's experience is part of the product.
  • Every buyer leaves informed and set up to succeed.
  • You're placing a life, not closing a sale.
03 · Standards
The experience you give families is what turns a one-time sale into a referral engine and premium pricing. It also protects the dogs — a well-prepared buyer is a better home. Qualifying out the wrong buyer is part of this standard, not a lost sale.
Standard 3

Positioning & Price

  • Premium is a standard, not a price tag.
  • Price on the value you deliver — and never apologize for it.
  • A fair price funds the standard of care the dogs deserve.
03 · Standards
Underpricing isn't humble — it starves the standard of care. When your program is genuinely premium (health, experience, systems), the price is simply the truth about the value. Help them connect: higher standards cost money, and a fair price is what pays for them.
Standard 4

Integrity

  • Registration, honesty, ethics — what you do when no one is watching.
  • No invented results, no hidden problems, no shortcuts that hurt a dog or a family.
  • Your reputation is the most valuable asset you own.
03 · Standards
Integrity is the standard that protects every other one. The industry runs on trust, and trust compounds or collapses. One dishonest placement can undo years of reputation. This is the standard that makes the BreedWise name mean something.
Part 4

Build Your
Systems

How to get the program out of your head and onto the page.
About 5 minutes. Now we make it practical — how to actually document a system so it runs without you.
The SOP formula

Trigger → Steps → Standard → Owner → Metric

  • Trigger — what starts it. Steps — the exact sequence.
  • Standard — what "done right" means. Owner — who's responsible.
  • Metric — the one number that proves it worked.
Document it once. Run it forever.
04 · Build
This is the template for every SOP in the program. Walk one example end to end — say, the go-home process: trigger (puppy hits 8 weeks), steps (vet check, microchip, pack assembled, buyer briefed), standard (nothing skipped), owner (you, for now), metric (buyer satisfaction). Five parts, and suddenly it's repeatable.
The discipline
Checklists over memory.
  • If it lives only in your head, you are the system — and the limit.
  • Written checklists don't get tired, distracted, or overwhelmed.
  • Every SOP you write buys back your time and protects the standard.
04 · Build
Surgeons and pilots use checklists not because they're forgetful but because stakes are high and memory fails under load — exactly like whelping night. Reframe writing SOPs as buying back your own time and protecting the dogs, not as bureaucracy.
Where to start

Don't systemize everything at once

  • Build the one system that's costing you the most — first.
  • Finish it, run it, then move to the next.
  • One system a month beats ten half-built ones.
04 · Build
The fastest way to fail at systems is to try to build all five at once and burn out. Pick the weakest/most painful one, build it fully, let it run, then move on. Momentum comes from finishing, not starting. This sets up the workshop directly.
Part 5

Measure
& Improve

Where this module plugs back into your Goal & Vision OS.
About 4 minutes, and the key callback to the Goal & Vision OS. Systems and standards aren't "set and forget" — they live in a weekly loop.
Make it real
A system you don't measure
is a hope.
  • Give each of the five systems one metric.
  • If the number moves, the system works. If it doesn't, fix the system — not yourself.
05 · Measure
One metric per system, no more — keep it simple enough to actually track. The reframe matters: when a number is off, it's a system problem to engineer, not a character flaw to feel bad about. That mindset keeps people improving instead of quitting.
Close the loop

This lives in your Goal & Vision OS

  • Your system metrics become your weekly KPIs.
  • The system you're building is your one signal this week.
  • Each week: review the numbers, pick the next move, commit it.
Standard → System → Measure → Improve. Every week.
05 · Measure
This is the integration moment — show them the exact handoff. Their five system metrics go straight into the KPIs section of the Goal & Vision OS; the system they're building this week is their "one signal." The loop — set the standard, build the system, measure it, improve it — runs every single week inside the tool they already have. That's how vision becomes a running machine.
