Gear for Content

You don’t need expensive gear to start. Here’s exactly what to buy at each stage — from a $20 mic to the cameras the pros use.
Keep it un-intimidating. Gear does not make the content — the message does. But the right basics make you look and sound professional. We’ll go tier by tier: start cheap with your phone, level up your audio and camera, then the pro setup. Buy as you grow, not all at once.
Before you buy anything

Start simple. Upgrade as you grow.

  • Your phone is enough to start. Modern phone cameras are excellent — don’t wait on a “real camera.”
  • Audio matters most. People forgive average video; they scroll past bad audio. Fix sound first.
  • Buy as you scale. Upgrade when your content is making money, not before.
Consistency beats gear. A daily phone creator out-earns a pro camera that never films.
Three rules before spending a dollar. One: your phone is more than enough to start. Two: audio matters most — a cheap mic beats an expensive camera with bad sound. Three: upgrade only as you scale and the content is earning. Consistency beats gear every time.

The pro tier. My favorite camera ever is the Sony FX30 — a cinematic vlogging camera. The ZV-E10 is a lighter, creator-focused alternative. For the cinematic, blurry-background look, add a fast prime lens — something like an 85mm or 135mm with a wide f1.4 to f1.8 aperture. Don’t get lost in the numbers: the lower the f-number, the more of that cinematic blur you get.
Whatever you buy

No matter what you buy, four rules. Audio first — fix sound before anything. Good light — just face a window; natural light outperforms expensive gear. Keep it simple — one camera, one lens, one mic, learned well. And show up consistently, because that beats every spec sheet.
The big idea

Buy as you grow

Start with your phone, a $20 mic, and a tripod. Upgrade your audio and camera as the content earns. The gear follows the growth — not the other way around.
