Part 1 · Section 01

Who we are & our story

BreedWise exists to make dog breeding a real, profitable, ethical business — and to raise the standard of the whole industry while we do it.

Most breeders love their dogs and lose money. They're great at whelping and weak on marketing, pricing, systems, and sales. BreedWise gives serious breeders the business education, the health & genetics knowledge, the tools, and the community to run a program they're proud of and that pays — without ever compromising the health of the dogs or the experience of the families who buy them. "Breed wiser": better decisions, better dogs, better business.

What we've become

  • A tiered ecosystem from $97/month education to high-ticket done-with-you coaching.
  • A community (hosted on Skool) where members learn, share wins, and ascend.
  • A veterinary-backed health & genetics arm, so the "wise" is earned.
  • A real sales & content engine — ads, funnels, scorecards, webinars — feeding a team that follows up and converts.
Our 90-day north star: a predictable, founder-independent sales engine. You are a core part of the top of that engine — often the first human a breeder talks to. The whole point of this manual is that you can run your seat without calling Austin for routine things.

Part 2 · Section 04

Your role, hours, pay & lead ownership

You convert attention into the right next step — across both the inbox you're assigned and the outreach you initiate.

What you own

  • Inbound: assigned new leads and replies in our DM inboxes — answered fast during your coverage window.
  • Outbound: initiating conversations with eligible, approved audiences (see Section 10 for the rules).
  • Qualifying every prospect, selling TPB directly, and setting qualified Academy/BOSS calls for Austin.
  • Recording every contact, stage, and next action in CC360, and sending a daily EOD report.

What you don't own

  • You don't close Academy/BOSS — you set them for Austin.
  • You don't quote unconfirmed prices or invent discounts/refunds.
  • You're not responsible for ad spend, content production, or fulfillment — but you are responsible for honest, complete handoffs.

Lead ownershipOne owner per lead, recorded in CC360. You own what you source or are assigned, until it's handed off with full context.

Part 3 · Section 05 · ★ Start here daily

Your first shift: start to finish

This is your daily operating checklist — the single most important page in the manual. Do it in this order, every shift.

1
Log in & confirm coverage. Open your approved accounts + CC360. Confirm today's coverage window and your backup contact.
2
Triage the inbox. Check unread inbound DMs, new replies to existing conversations, and new lead assignments.
3
Prioritize by intent. High-intent prospects and scheduled callbacks before new outbound. Speed on hot leads beats volume on cold ones.
4
Respond in context. Use what the prospect actually said. Don't repeat questions an automation or teammate already answered — read the thread first.
5
Qualify & route. Recommend the right next step (Section 06). Record the contact's current stage and next action in CC360.
6
Follow up & confirm. Clear due follow-ups and confirm upcoming appointments so they show.
7
Check ownership. Scan for duplicate contact ownership or unresolved handoffs; resolve or flag them.
8
Close out. Submit the EOD report and assign any time-sensitive open conversations to the next available owner.

Part 4 · Section 06

Qualify, route & handle objections

Learn the shape of a great conversation, then sound like a human inside it. Gather facts; don't improvise breeding ethics.

The principle

Money qualifies the tier — it does not qualify the person. Your job is to gather the relevant facts and route to the right next step. Where fit touches breeding ethics, health testing, or welfare, you collect the facts and refer to a qualified reviewer — you don't rule on them yourself.

Routing decision table

Prospect situationDefault next step
Wants self-directed education & business toolsExplain TPB, send the approved checkout
Needs structured coaching & accountabilityQualify for an Academy call (Austin)
Needs intensive business-systems implementationQualify for a BOSS discussion (Austin)
Interested but not ready to buyOffer the relevant free resource; set an appropriate follow-up
Needs individual veterinary diagnosis / urgent medical guidanceEscalate appropriately; no clinical advice, no sales pressure
Program raises welfare / integrity concernsDocument the facts; refer for review — don't rule on it yourself
Existing customer needs helpRoute to Jennifer / member support

Conversation shape

1
Open — earn the reply. Personalized, specific, no pitch. Reference what they engaged with.
2
Qualify — get them talking. Where's their program now? What do they want? What's in the way? Listen more than you type.
3
Diagnose — name the gap. Reflect the distance between where they are and where they want to be.
4
Route — the right step. TPB in the DM; "let's get you a quick call with Austin" for serious Academy/BOSS fits.
5
Confirm & log. Lock time, set reminder, record stage + next action, tag ownership to you.

Starter language (make it yours)

Opener: "Hey [name] — saw you breed [breed]. Quick one: are you running this as a real business yet, or still mostly covering costs?"
Route to TPB: "Fastest first step for where you're at is our membership — $97/mo, exactly the playbook for that. Want the link?"
Set the call: "This is past what I can solve in DMs. You'd get more from a quick call with Austin — I've got [time]. Want me to lock it in?"

Objection handling (honest reframes, never pressure)

ObjectionApproved approach
"Too expensive"Clarify tier fit — maybe TPB is the right first step. Never discount; frame value vs. the cost of staying stuck.
"I need to think about it"Normalize it, find the real question, set a concrete follow-up time. Don't chase.
"I already do this"Ask what's working and what isn't — the gap is the opening, not an argument.
"Will it work for my breed?"It's business & systems, breed-agnostic. If unsure, book the call rather than over-claim.
"I need to ask my spouse/partner"Respect it; get both on the call or set a follow-up. No pressure.

The full qualification standard is a separate document — ask Austin/Regina for the deep version when a case is borderline.

Part 6 · Section 08

Follow-ups, no-shows, nurture & opt-outs

Most of the money is in the follow-up — done with respect, not with a pressure quota.

Approved follow-up examples

Pricing question, went quiet: "Hey [name], did that pricing make sense for where you're at — or want me to walk you through which option fits best?"
Delayed decision: "No rush at all. Want me to check back [specific day], or is there a question I can answer now?"
Ghosted (gentle, final): "I'll close this out so I'm not crowding your inbox — if the timing's better later, just say the word and I'll pick it right back up."
No-show: "Looks like we missed each other — totally happens. Here's the next opening: [time]. Want me to grab it for you?"
Seasonally inactive breeder: "Sounds like you're between litters. Want me to check back when you're planning the next one? I'll set a reminder."

Opt-outs — non-negotiable

If a prospect asks for no further contact, stop immediately and update their opt-out status in CC360. Never re-add them to outreach. Respect every platform's messaging rules and each prospect's stated preference.

No aggressive follow-up quotas. We do not spam. Cadence serves the prospect's preference and the platform's rules — not a number on a scoreboard. A respectful "no" protected is worth more than a forced "yes."
Part 7 · Section 09

Claims, privacy & the escalation matrix

A few lines protect the brand, the breeder, the dogs — and you. These are non-negotiable.

What you may claim

  • What each offer includes (approved wording), that a real repro vet backs our education, and honest member outcomes you can support.

What you must never do

  • Never diagnose, prescribe, or give clinical instructions for an animal.
  • Never guarantee puppy survival or health outcomes.
  • Never guarantee profits or income.
  • Never promise an unapproved discount or refund.
  • Never claim to be a veterinarian.
  • Never disclose confidential member or prospect information.
  • Never invent results, fake scarcity, or misrepresent the offer.

Part 7 · Section 10

Messaging compliance

Before you message at volume, know the boundaries. These protect our accounts and our reputation.

Approved audiencesOnly the audiences & lead sources you're assigned. Austin/ops to define the exact list. No scraping or buying lists.
Account accessUse only the approved accounts you're given. Never use personal accounts for company outreach.
Consent & opt-outHonor every opt-out instantly; record it in CC360. Don't message people who've asked to stop.
Platform & spam rulesFollow each platform's limits and anti-spam rules. Don't blast identical copy; personalize. Protect the accounts.
AutomationOnly approved tools/automations. Confirm what's permitted. Never run unapproved bots or mass-senders.
When unsure, ask before you send. A banned account costs the whole team its pipeline. "Message at volume" means as much qualified, compliant outreach as the rules and your hours allow — not spam.
