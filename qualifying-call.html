NEPQ · 7-belief syntax

Qualify in 10 minutes. Book the fit.

Go top to bottom. Read the gold lines out loud. Write down their short answers. The box at the bottom tells you if you should book them. Talk like one breeder to another — you're not selling, you're just checking the fit. Build the dream before you talk money. Let them talk most of the time. Gold lines are the must-asks. The faint extra lines are only if you have time in 10 minutes.

No selling · build the dream · then check the fit Ask nicely → give context → then the question Book if: they can pay + they want it now + they can decide
0

Open & relax them

breeder to breeder
Take the pressure off. Make them feel safe. No selling.
"Hey [name], thanks for hopping on. Before we start — I'm not here to sell you. I just want to learn about your dogs and your program. Then we'll see if we're a good fit to help. Sound okay?"
"What made you reach out? What were you hoping to fix?"
1

What they want to fix

core
Find the real problem in their words. Stay here until you get it.
"When you look at your program right now, what bugs you the most? If you could fix one thing today, what would it be?"
"Is there anything else getting in the way too?"
2

Their program

context · credibility
Learn the basics fast. Show them you know dogs.
"Tell me about your dogs. What breed? How many litters a year? What do your puppies sell for?"
"How do people find you and buy a puppy right now? Walk me through it."
Drop in a word about health testing or raising pups. It shows them you know dogs.
3

The real picture

dig · gently
Get the story. Then get the real numbers. Stay friendly.
"Tell me more about that. What has it looked like lately?"
"Let's talk numbers. In the last two months, how many puppies did you sell? For how much? Is that where you want to be, or are you selling too cheap?"
"How long has it been like this?"
"When it stays like this, what does it cost you? Is it the money? The stress every litter? Not getting paid what your dogs are worth? What's the hardest part?"
4

What's held it back

doubt
"You clearly know dogs really well. So what do you think has held the money side back?"
"Have you tried to fix it before? A class, a mentor, or on your own? Anything now?"
5

Why now

urgency
Find out why now. Don't push. Breeders can tell when you push.
"You've known about this for a while. Why do something about it now? What changed?"
"Any other reason it's time now?"
"If nothing changed for the next year or two, how would you feel?"
5
Low urgency. Keep sharing the dream, or follow up later. Don't book a weak lead onto the closer's calendar.
6

The dream

desire · lead here
Build the dream first. Think waitlist, respect, and pride. Money comes later.
"Now the fun part. If we fixed this and your program ran great, what would it look like? What kind of breeder do you want to be known as?"
"A full waitlist? Top prices for your pups? Picking the best homes instead of chasing buyers?"
"What would that give you? More time with your family and dogs? Less stress? A program you're proud of?"
7

The number & real financial fit

qualify hard
Don't say a price. But "yes" has to be a real yes. Anyone can say maybe. They don't know it's $4,000 to $8,000. Show them the size, then check if they can really pay. Only mark Yes if their answers prove it.
"Picture that dream program. How much money would it need to make for you to feel like you made it? What's the number?"
"Why that number? What does it mean to you?"
Show the size, no price: "I won't give you a price today. That's for the next call with [Austin]. But I'll be honest so I don't waste your time. A real fix costs real money — about the price of one or two nice puppies. Most breeders split it into monthly payments."
Where the money comes from: "If this was clearly the right move, where would the money come from? Savings? Your dog account? A card?"
Monthly room: "If it was monthly payments, could you handle something like a car payment for a few months? Or would that be really hard right now?"
Have they spent before: "Have you spent money on your program before? A stud fee, health tests, a good dog? About how much?"
Why you ask: "I only ask all this so I don't book you a call for a plan you can't pay for. That wastes your time and his."
Can't pay right now. Be kind and honest. Keep in touch. Don't book a call for a plan they can't pay for.
8

Who's in on it

decision
Ask once. Nicely. Treat the partner like a teammate.
"If you decide to do this, is it your call alone? Or is there a partner you'd want in on it?"
If partner: "Makes sense. Should we get them on the next call too, so you both hear the plan?"
Get the partner on the next call. Say it's "so you both hear the plan." Book both.
9

Wrap up & book

book the fit
"This has been a great talk. I feel like I get where you're at and where you want to go. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should know?"
"The breeders who win fastest put in a few hours a week. Does that feel doable?"
Book it: "Here's the truth. From all this, I really think we can help you hit [their goal]. The next step is a call with [Austin]. He'll show you the exact plan. No pressure, just the plan. Let's book it right now. What does your week look like?"
!

When to use Goals-First

read me
Use Problems-First most of the time. Switch to this only when:
· They're brand new to this, or making a big change.
· They can't name the real problem, or say "I don't know where to start."
· They sound confused — asking "what do you want?" clears it up.
1

Flip to the dream

If they're stuck: "Let's flip it. Let's start with the dream. If everything went great, what would you build here?"
2

The dream & the fit

desire first
"What kind of breeder do you want to be known as?" · "How much would it need to make for you to feel like you made it? Why that number?" · "Besides money, what do you want it to give you?"
Then: "If we showed you a clear path and it was worth it, could you pay to make it happen this year?"
Use the same fields above (the dream, money goal, personal goal, money check) — scroll up or fill after the call.
3

Where they are now

"Where are you today? What are you working with now?" Look for the gap between now and the dream.
"What do you think has stopped you from getting there on your own?"
"Tried anything before? Anyone helping now? Who helps you decide?"
4

The two keys

authority + insight
Sound like the expert. Give real help. Show them the gap.
"Can I share something? To build a program that pays what your dogs are worth, you need two things:"
1 — A brand people happily pay top dollar for. 2 — A steady way to fill your waitlist — not luck each litter.
"On the brand side, where are you at? And filling your list — do you have a system, or do you need help there?"
They almost always need help filling the list. That's your opening — then book the call.
5

Wrap up & book

"This has been great. Anything I didn't ask that I should know?" → then book the call with [Austin] to show them the plan.
+

Setter notes

Stuck? Easy line: "I think I have what I need. From what you told me, we can really help. Where do you want to go from here? I can show you how we'd help you hit [goal], if you want."
