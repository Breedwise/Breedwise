The Qualification Call

A framework to reference, not a script to read. Your job: uncover pain, test fit, measure urgency, and route. You finish every call with one of three outcomes.

Your mental model (memorize this, not the words)
What hurts? → How serious is it? → What do they actually need? → Why now? → Where do I send them?
Don't interrogate. Don't coach. Don't pitch early. If they give you what you need in three answers, don't ask eight more. Deepen the diagnosis, never give the prescription. This is a conversation you're steering, not a form you're filling.

Lower resistance. This is a fit call, not a pitch.

Your move

0:45–2:30

Pain

Find the consequence, not just the topic.

Your move

  • Open broad, then go to the single biggest struggle. Let them talk.
  • When an answer is vague, make it concrete before moving on.
  • End this block on impact: what is the problem actually costing them.
Words you could use

"Tell me about your program. How long have you been breeding, and what are you working on right now?"

"What's the biggest thing you're struggling with?"

"How is that affecting your program right now?" (the key one)

Listen for

If the answer is vague, pin it down

"I need more buyers" → "Are you not getting enough inquiries, or getting them and struggling to turn them into committed buyers?"

"I need to make more money" → "Is it pricing, expenses, filling litters, or not really knowing what you make after everything's accounted for?"

"I want to improve my program" → "What specifically makes you feel something needs to change?"

2:30–4:00

Fit

Where they are + education vs. implementation.

Your move

  • Get their scale and whether they're trying to grow or comfortable.
  • Ask what they've already tried (tells you how aware and how stuck they are).
  • The pivotal question: do they need better information and a plan, or do they know what to change and want hands-on help implementing it?
Words you could use

"How many litters are you typically having a year right now?"

"Are you actively trying to grow or improve, or pretty comfortable where it is?"

"What have you already tried to fix [their problem]?"

"Do you mainly need better information and a clear plan, or do you already know a lot about what needs to change and want more hands-on help actually implementing it?"

4:00–5:00

Urgency

"Nice to have" or "I'm tired of this"?

Your move

  • Two questions, don't overdo it. You're testing motivation, not manufacturing it.
  • If they're clearly motivated, test timing (now vs. later).
Words you could use

"Why is fixing this important to you now?"

"What happens if nothing really changes over the next 6 to 12 months?"

"If there's a good path to solving this, are you looking to start now, or is this more of a later thing?"

Listen for

"it would be nice" (low)"I need to fix this" (high)
~5:00

Give value before you ask

Make them feel helped. Then the next step is a gift, not a toll.
This is the fix for "we're giving nothing and then asking for money." The call is the value — you just helped them name the real problem, which most breeders have never had anyone do. Say it out loud, give one useful insight, and leave them better off whether or not they buy. Do this and the recommendation feels like the obvious next step, not a sales pitch.

Three moves, in this order

  • 1 · Reflect the real problem. Play it back as clarity. That naming is the gift.
  • 2 · Give one generous insight. A single reframe that costs you nothing and makes them feel helped. Never the full prescription.
  • 3 · Leave them with a free next step — send the free 2-Minute Breeder Scorecard, whether or not they buy today.
Words you could use

1. "Here's what I'm actually hearing: it's not [surface issue], it's [the real bottleneck]. Naming that is half the battle — most breeders never get that clear."

2. "The good news is that's fixable, and it's usually a [positioning / pricing / follow-up] thing, not a 'work harder' thing."

3. "Either way, let me send you our free 2-Minute Breeder Scorecard so you walk away with a quick read on where your program actually stands today."

Before routing

Let them self-select. Never state the price.

Your move

  • Never name the Academy price. You're reading a signal, not quoting a number. Austin handles price on the call.

Skill

Deepen, don't prescribe

When you hear a problem, your instinct will be to solve it. Don't. Mirror it back sharper and keep diagnosing.

They say: "I get 30 inquiries but nobody places a deposit."

✗ Don't spend 4 minutes teaching them a sales process.

✓ "Got it, so it's not getting attention, it's turning the attention you already have into buyers who commit. How long has that been happening?"

That's setting: deepening the diagnosis without giving away the prescription.

"If Regina puts someone on my calendar, I want to take that call."
That's the standard for this seat. Your metric is routing accuracy, not booking volume. Five genuinely qualified prospects beat ten mediocre ones.
