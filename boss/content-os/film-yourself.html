Film Yourself

The exact step-by-step for talking-to-camera content with a teleprompter and B-roll — the authentic, at-work style that goes viral.
Set it up. This is the format that builds authority faster than anything else: you, speaking to camera, with B-roll of you actually working behind it. Because it’s authentic and shows real work, it tends to go viral. We’ll walk the whole workflow step by step, using tools you already have today.
Why this goes viral

Authentic beats polished

The over-produced ad
  • Feels like a commercial
  • People scroll past it
  • Builds no real connection
Forgettable
You, real, at work
  • Speaking honestly to camera
  • B-roll of you doing the work
  • Builds trust and authority fast
This is what spreads
The algorithm and the audience both reward authenticity. A talking-head video where people see you doing the real work — raising the dogs, in the whelping room, at the kennel — builds trust in a way a polished ad never will. That realness is exactly why this format goes viral.

Four things: your phone and clip-on mic on a tripod; the Instagram Edits app, which is free and has a built-in teleprompter; a short script with a hook, value, and a call to action; and B-roll — clips of you actually working. That’s it. Everything here is free or already in your pocket.
The workflow

Seven steps, start to post

  • Script it — hook, value, CTA
  • Load the teleprompter in Edits
  • Set up the shot — tripod, mic, light
  • Film yourself reading to the lens
  • Capture B-roll of you working
  • Edit — overlay B-roll + captions
  • Hook & post
Here’s the whole workflow at a glance, seven steps: script it, load the teleprompter, set up the shot, film yourself reading to the lens, capture B-roll of you working, edit by overlaying that B-roll with captions, then nail the hook and post. We’ll break down each one.
Steps 1 & 2

Script it, then load the teleprompter

Hook — a scroll-stopping first line (first 3 seconds)
Value — the one point you’re teaching or showing
CTA — tell them exactly what to do next
Keep it tight — 30 to 60 seconds of talking
Paste into Edits — open the teleprompter tool
Set the scroll speed — match your natural pace
Tip: use the Content OS scripting tool to generate your script, then paste it in.
Step one: write a short script — a hook in the first three seconds, one clear piece of value, and a call to action. Keep it to 30 to 60 seconds. Step two: open the Instagram Edits app, go to the teleprompter, paste your script in, and set the scroll speed to match how you naturally talk. The scripting tool in this module can write the whole script for you — just paste it in.
Steps 3 & 4

Set up, then film to the lens

Tripod — phone steady at eye level
Mic on — clip the lav mic, check the audio
Light — face a window; light in front, not behind
Frame — you centered, a little headroom
Look at the lens — not at the script, at the camera
Energy up — speak like you’re talking to a friend
The teleprompter sits right by the lens, so your eyes stay on camera.
Step three, set up: phone on the tripod at eye level, clip on the mic and check your audio, face a window so the light is on you not behind you, and frame yourself centered with a little headroom. Step four, film: read the teleprompter but keep your eyes on the lens — the prompter sits right next to it — and bring real energy, like you’re talking to a friend.
Step 5 — the secret sauce

Capture B-roll of you working

This is what makes it feel authentic instead of staged. Film yourself actually doing the work.
Feeding & careIn the whelping roomPlaying with pups Cleaning & setupHealth checksTrainingGo-home prep
Grab 10-15 seconds of each. You’ll lay it over your talking in the next step.
Step five is the secret sauce: B-roll of you actually working. Grab short clips — feeding, in the whelping room, playing with puppies, cleaning, health checks, training, prepping for go-home day. Ten to fifteen seconds of each is plenty. This real, at-work footage is what makes the video feel authentic and is a huge reason it spreads.
Step 6

Edit — overlay the B-roll

Drop in your talking clip as the base layer
Overlay B-roll on top while you keep talking
Cut the dead air — tighten every pause
Add captions — most people watch on mute
Keep your audio playing under the B-roll
Trim to the point — shorter holds attention
All of this happens inside the free Edits app — no other software needed.
Step six, edit, all inside Edits: your talking clip is the base, then you overlay the B-roll on top while your voice keeps playing underneath. Cut every pause and dead moment, add captions because most people watch on mute, and trim it tight. Shorter videos hold attention and get finished, which the algorithm rewards.
Step 7

Hook, caption, post

First 3 seconds — lead with your strongest hook
On-screen hook text — reinforce it visually
Caption — add context and a question
CTA — comment, DM, or link in bio
Post consistently — same style, on repeat
Repurpose — Reel, Short, TikTok, FB
Step seven, post: the first three seconds must lead with your strongest hook, reinforced with on-screen text. Write a caption that adds context and asks a question to drive comments, and end with a clear call to action. Then post consistently in the same style, and repurpose the same video as a Reel, a Short, a TikTok, and a Facebook post.
