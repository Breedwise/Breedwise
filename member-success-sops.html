SOP 02

Community Management

Owner: Jennifer · Keep The Profitable Breeder professional, helpful, respectful and safe — without over-moderating honest discussion.
Core rule
Moderate behavior, not opinions.
Members may disagree, challenge ideas, share different experiences, ask uncomfortable questions, and discuss industry problems. Disagreement ≠ rule violation.
1

Leave it up

Do not remove a post/comment just because someone:

  • Disagrees with BreedWise or another member
  • Shares constructive criticism
  • Has a different breeding philosophy or experience
  • Asks a controversial or difficult question
  • Expresses frustration respectfully
2

Remove + DM the member

Remove content that includes:

  • Personal attacks, bullying, harassment, targeted insults
  • Spam, unauthorized promotion, solicitation, selling
  • Sharing someone's private info without permission
  • Explicit/inappropriate content unrelated to breeder education
  • Repeated inflammatory behavior meant to create conflict
  • Clear community-rule violations
  • Medical instructions presented as diagnosis/treatment for another member's dog
Medical line: members can share experiences and ask educational questions. If it crosses into diagnosing or directing treatment for an individual animal, remove/redirect toward appropriate veterinary care.
Message Jennifer sends (privately)
Hey [Name] — I removed your recent post/comment because [specific reason]. We absolutely welcome questions and different perspectives inside The Profitable Breeder. We just need conversations to stay within our community guidelines. You're welcome to repost/rephrase it without [specific issue]. If you have any questions, message me here. — Jennifer

Always moderate privately. Never publicly embarrass the member.

3

Remove immediately

Jennifer does not wait for Austin to remove obvious:

  • Spam/scams
  • Threats
  • Severe harassment
  • Privacy violations/doxxing
  • Explicitly inappropriate content
  • Dangerous content needing immediate action

Remove first. Document it. Notify Austin if serious.

4

Escalation

Normal disagreementLeave it.
First rule violationRemove if necessary + private warning.
Repeated violationRemove + final warning + document pattern.
Serious/repeated behaviorSuspend + notify Austin.
Severe/persistent behaviorPermanent ban with Austin's approval.

If immediate action is needed to protect the community, Jennifer can suspend first and notify Austin immediately after.

5

Documentation

Any removal, warning, suspension, or ban is recorded internally:

This prevents decisions from being based on memory.

6

When to ask Austin

If Jennifer isn't sure whether something actually violates a rule: don't guess — screenshot it and ask Austin. Also escalate threats, legal concerns, serious member disputes, repeated misconduct, refund/payment conflicts, or anything that could materially affect BreedWise's reputation.

SOP 03

Inactive Member

Owner: Jennifer · Spot members who've stopped engaging, understand why, and help them take one clear next step before they quietly cancel.
Core rule
Don't chase activity. Restore direction.
The goal isn't logins for their own sake — it's helping them remember why they joined and what to do next.
1

What counts as inactive?

New member

No onboarding/Scorecard within 7 days of joining.

Existing member

No meaningful activity/progress for 14+ days.

High risk

No progress 30+ days, or says they're overwhelmed, stuck, too busy, or considering canceling.

Jennifer reviews inactive members once per week.

2

Check before contacting

What did they join to accomplish?
Did they complete onboarding?
Did they complete the Scorecard?
What was their #1 priority?
What have they completed?
Last meaningful interaction?

Never send a generic "Hey! Haven't seen you lately!" Use what BreedWise already knows.

3

7-day new member follow-up

Hey [Name]! Just checking in because I noticed you haven't finished your [Onboarding/Scorecard] yet. You mentioned that [PERSONAL GOAL/CHALLENGE] was important to you when you joined. Your best next step is to complete [ONE ACTION]. Once that's done, send me a message and I'll help point you toward what to focus on next. — Jennifer

Give them ONE next step. Not five lessons to catch up on.

4

14-day inactive follow-up

Hey [Name]! I wanted to check in with you. Last time we connected, your main priority was [PRIORITY]. How are things going with that? If you've gotten stuck or life/breeding has gotten busy, tell me where you're at and I'll help you figure out the best next step. — Jennifer

The objective is to start a conversation, not guilt them into engagement.

5

If they respond — identify the blocker

"I don't know what to do"

Reconnect to their #1 priority + one next action.

"I'm overwhelmed"

Simplify: "Let's ignore everything else — your next step is ______."

"I've been too busy"

Ask when they realistically want to restart; set a follow-up date.

"I don't think this is helping"

Ask what they expected + what's missing. Document. Escalate to Austin if appropriate.

"I'm thinking about canceling"

Don't pressure. Understand why, document the reason, help where appropriate, follow the cancellation process if they decide to.

Needs hands-on implementation

May be an Academy signal — follow the Ascension SOP. Don't turn every inactive member into a sale.

6

30-day high-risk follow-up

Hey [Name] — wanted to reach out personally one more time because I don't want your membership to turn into something you're paying for but not using. Your original priority was [PRIORITY]. If that's still important, reply here and I'll help you identify the simplest next step. If your priorities have changed, that's completely fine too — just let me know where things stand. — Jennifer

This should feel like Member Success, not retention pressure.

7

If they don't respond

After the 30-day outreach, do not endlessly chase. Record:

Status: Inactive — No Response
Last outreach
Original priority
Last known progress
Next review

They remain a member unless they cancel. Keep normal community communication; stop repeated personal outreach until there's a reason to re-engage.

8

Reactivation

When they come back, don't make them feel behind.

Welcome back! You don't need to catch up on everything. Last time we worked together, your priority was [PRIORITY]. Is that still the biggest thing you want to improve?

Yes → continue from there.   No → update priority + point to the next step. If they've been gone long enough that their situation changed, have them retake the Scorecard.

SOP 04

Scorecard Analyzer

Owner: Jennifer · Turn every completed Scorecard into a standardized insight, a next-step conversation, and a potential Academy handoff — in 3–5 minutes.
Core rule
The Scorecard shows where to investigate — not what to sell.
Jennifer turns scores into a useful conversation and spots an implementation signal. Regina qualifies the sales opportunity. There is no "score under 60% = Academy lead."

A

How Jennifer reads the Scorecard

AreaA lower score MAY indicateExplore
These are conversation prompts, not conclusions. A low Demand score doesn't mean "you have a marketing problem." It means "let's understand what's happening with demand."
B

The disconnect patterns

1 · Low score + member knows it

Easy. They recognize it. Explore impact + urgency + what they've tried.

2 · Low score + different stated priority

Create clarity, not pain: "You said more buyers is the priority — but Litter Economics stood out. How confident are you in what each litter actually costs and makes?"

3 · Business strong, systems weak

Has demand/buyers/pricing but lacks repeatable preparation. Explore whether growth outpaced the systems.

4 · Systems strong, business weak

"Your breeding prep is one of your stronger areas; most gaps are on the business side. Does that match your experience?"

5 · Everything low

Don't dump seven problems. "If we improved just ONE area in 90 days, which makes the biggest difference?" May need TPB clarity first.

6 · Everything looks good

Don't invent a problem. "What's the biggest thing keeping your program from where you want it next?" May be a happy TPB member — that's fine.

C

The 4 sales signals & the formula

  • Gap — meaningful difference between where they are and where they want to be.
  • Impact — the gap is actually creating a problem (time, stress, lost buyers, low margin, uncertainty).
  • Urgency — a reason it matters now (upcoming breeding/litter, puppies available, pricing change, scaling).
  • Implementation need — the Academy signal: "I understand what needs to change — I need help building/fixing it."

Gap + Impact + Urgency + Implementation → potential Regina handoff. Gap alone → a TPB conversation, not a sales conversation.

The formula to train Jennifer on
Score → Pattern → Question → Impact → Need → Next step
Data says what? → What stands out? → Does the member agree? → Does it matter? → Education or implementation? → TPB (education/clarity) or Regina (implementation + impact + urgency).
