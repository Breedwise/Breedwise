Who qualifies — and who doesn't.

Regina, this is the bar. We're building BreedWise for the long-term vision: a community of genuinely quality breeders we can give the best head start. Money never buys someone past this standard. The goal isn't to reject people who need us — it's to protect what being in the program means.

The gate, in one sentence
We take breeders who do it right — or sincerely want to learn to — proven by what they can show and name, not by what they claim or what they'd pay.
Clears the standard below and can invest in the right tier → qualify & route.  Fails on ethics or honesty → decline.  Short on money only → route down a tier, never reject.
A website build is not the program — so we're never contradicting ourselves. Our program is one ladder: TPB → Academy → BOSS (diagnosis → guided implementation → hands-on implementation). A website build is a separate service, not one of those tiers — we can build a breeder a site without it placing them in the program or past this standard. Even for BOSS, where we'd build systems like a site hands-on, the breeder has to clear this standard first. A web client is not an automatic program fit. That's not a contradiction — it's the standard doing its job.
A

Their WHY

Passion for the dogs and the craft — not a get-rich scheme.
✓
Good: Loves the dogs and wants a healthy, reputable program with a sustainable income from doing it well.
✕
Not good: "I heard there's money in it." Dogs-as-inventory, puppies-as-product, no real care where they end up.
?
Ask: "Why do you breed?" and "What does a great program look like for you in 3 years?" → Health, dogs and families first = good. Money first, dogs barely mentioned = red.
B

The dogs & registration

✓
Good: Dogs are registered (AKC / UKC / legitimate breed registry — for designer breeds, the parent breeds are registered) with real, traceable pedigrees. Or they're new and openly willing to do it properly.
✕
Not good: Untraceable stock, no idea of lineage, "papers are a scam so I don't bother," and unwilling to register or verify.
?
Ask: "Are your dogs registered, and with whom? Can you share the pedigree?" If no → "Why not — and are you open to doing it right?" Willing = fine. Refuses + untraceable = red.
C

Breeding practice — purpose vs careless crossing

✓
Good: Purposeful pairings to a standard — a clear goal for health, structure, temperament, or a defined ethical market, with tested and registered stock behind it.
✕
Not good: Careless crossing — mixing whatever two dogs they have, no testing, no registration, no purpose beyond "it sells." "Oops litters" dressed up as intentional.
?
Ask: "Walk me through your last pairing — why those two dogs?" A real answer has a reason (improve X, a health or breed goal). "They were both here / people want that mix," with no testing = red.
On crossbreeding — the strict read. Crossing breeds is not automatically a fit, and we scrutinize it harder, not softer. A designer/doodle program can qualify only when it's purposeful, health-tested, and built on registered parent stock to a real standard. If it's careless, untested, unregistered, or just "what sells," it's a no — no matter who it is or what they'd pay. We're building the long-term vision, not grandfathering in how anyone currently operates.
D

Health testing — real vs claimed

This is the one people fake most. Catch it with specifics.
✓
Good: Can name the actual tests, the dogs they ran them on, and recent results — or is new and honestly says "not yet, but that's exactly what I want to learn."
✕
Not good: Says "yes, we health test" but can't name a single panel, result, or certificate. That's not a testing gap — it's dishonesty, and the dishonesty is the disqualifier.
🔎 The bluff-catcher — ask all three
  1. "Which specific tests do you run — OFA hips/elbows, Embark or a genetic panel, PennHIP, cardiac, eyes (CAER), patellas?"
  2. "What were your last pairing's results — and do you have the OFA numbers or the Embark report?"
  3. "What result would make you NOT breed a dog?"
Read it: a real tester answers all three easily and can send proof. A pretender gets vague, deflects, or has no idea what they'd cull for. Vague on all three = they don't actually test.
Hold the line both ways. "Doesn't test yet but is honest and eager to learn" = good — that's exactly who we teach. "Claims they test but clearly doesn't" = not good — they lied. Honesty is the line, not the current result.
E

Honesty

✓
Good: Straight, specific answers; admits what they don't do yet.
✕
Not good: Inflates, dodges, or tells you what you want to hear. If they'll fake it to us, they'll fake it to buyers.
?
Tell: specific + consistent = honest. Confident but can't back it up = not.
F

Coachability & standards

✓
Good: Wants to be held to a higher standard; open to changing how they operate after the education.
✕
Not good: "I already know it all." Wants the BreedWise name and credibility without doing the work.
✕

Hard disqualifiers — no tier, no budget fixes these

  • Puppy-mill behavior or intent — volume over welfare, no vet care, inhumane conditions, and no willingness to change.
  • Dogs as a cash machine — zero welfare standard and unwilling to adopt one.
  • Dishonesty / fraud — lies about their operation, health, or pedigree; scam behavior.
  • Careless crossing for profit — untested, unregistered, no purpose, and no intent to change.
  • Refuses coaching or any standard — wants the logo, not the work.
  • Abusive, hostile, or manipulative toward you or the team.
  • Illegal operation they won't bring into compliance (licensing, welfare law).
  • Flipper / broker reselling others' puppies with no program and no ethics.
  • Wants help cutting corners — deceiving buyers or dodging health standards.
Willing but currently unethical? An honest beginner who's simply under-educated is a yes — that's who we raise up. Someone already running a bad operation (mill-scale, careless crossing for cash, fake testing) who just says they'll change is a no until they show real change. Fraud and cruelty are never salvageable by a promise.
$

The money line

Ability to pay qualifies the tier — never the person. Someone who'd gladly write a check but fails A–F is a no. We don't sell education to someone who'll use it to run a slicker version of a bad operation. We'd rather have a broke, honest, first-year breeder than a wealthy one breeding carelessly for cash. Payment flexibility (plans/terms) is offered to anyone who clears the ethics bar — it sets the terms, not whether they're in.
♥

Never hold these against them

New / small / pre-first-litter · not testing yet but honest and willing · low revenue today · past mistakes they own and want to fix · not polished or tech-savvy · nervous on the call · needs a payment plan · breed, location, background, age (adult), accent or English fluency, or any protected characteristic. The program exists to raise the standard — "not there yet" is never the no. "Won't get there" is.
